Comment-to-DM vs link-in-bio: A link-in-bio is passive, leaving buyers to hunt through your bio. Comment-to-DM is active. It drops the listing, your showing calendar, or a home-valuation form into the inbox and saves the lead, so a hot listing stops leaking buyers and sellers.

Seller leads: the "what's my home worth" funnel

Seller leads are the prize in real estate, and Instagram is full of them. Every homeowner who follows you is a potential listing.

The classic hook is the home valuation lead magnet. Post a market update or a just sold win, then ask viewers to comment for a free home-value estimate. Even FSBO (for sale by owner) followers are prime listing leads.

Comment VALUE, and the DM sends your home-valuation form or CMA request, and captures the address and contact. One Reel can fill your pipeline with listing appointments.

Fair Housing applies to your DMs too

This is the part agents must get right, and most automation guides skip it. The Fair Housing Act governs your captions and automated DMs, not just paid ads.

It bars any housing ad that signals a preference based on a protected class: race, color, religion, sex, disability, familial status, or national origin. That includes casual lines like "perfect for a young family" or "great for bachelors." Meta learned this the hard way, settling with the U.S. DOJ in 2022 and paying the maximum Fair Housing Act penalty over housing-ad discrimination.

Because your disclaimer and your wording send on every automated DM, write them once and write them right.

Do
  • Describe the property, not the ideal buyer
  • Include your license number and brokerage
  • Add an Equal Housing Opportunity note where required
  • Treat every inquiry the same way
Don't
  • "Perfect for families" or "ideal for singles"
  • Reference religion, ethnicity, or "safe" areas
  • Screen or steer leads by protected class
  • Skip required license disclosure

The same honesty rule applies abroad. In India, the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 (RERA) requires agents to register and display their RERA registration number in property ads, and penalizes misleading listings. Wherever you operate, describe the property accurately and follow local disclosure rules.

No, as long as the tool uses Meta's official API and only messages people who commented, replied, or DMed first. Password bots, scrapers, and cold DMs are what break the rules.

Yes. A keyword like VALUE sends your home-valuation form and captures the address and contact, turning a market-update Reel into listing appointments.
