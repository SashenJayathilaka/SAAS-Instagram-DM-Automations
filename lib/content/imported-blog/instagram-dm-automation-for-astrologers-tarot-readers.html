The disclaimer that keeps you out of trouble

This is the part most automation guides skip, and it matters more for readers than for any other niche.

Add a "for entertainment purposes only" line to your DMs. It is standard practice across tarot and astrology services, and some U.S. jurisdictions regulate fortune-telling, so a few even require it. Automation makes it effortless: write it once, it sends every time.

Do
  • "For entertainment purposes only" in the DM
  • "Readings don't replace medical, legal, or financial advice"
  • Keep bookings 18+ where required
  • "#ad" before any affiliate deck or course link
Don't
  • Promise guaranteed outcomes
  • State health, legal, or money predictions as fact
  • Drop an undisclosed paid product link
  • Ignore your local rules

Frequently Asked Questions

Is automating DMs against Instagram's rules?
No, as long as the tool uses Meta's official API and only messages people who commented, replied, or DMed first. Password bots, scrapers, and cold DMs are what break the rules.
Do automated DMs land in the message-requests folder?
No. Because the person engaged first, your reply lands in your normal conversation thread, not a cold request folder.
Do I need a disclaimer in my DMs?
Many readers add "for entertainment purposes only" and note that readings don't replace medical, legal, or financial advice. Some jurisdictions regulate fortune-telling, so check your local rules. This isn't legal advice.
Can I send a different horoscope per zodiac sign?
Yes. Use a keyword per sign, or one keyword that asks for the sign, and the DM sends the matching monthly horoscope.
Can I sell birth charts, decks, or courses through DMs?
Yes. A keyword like CHART or SHOP delivers your product or affiliate link. Add "#ad" before any affiliate link to stay FTC-compliant.
